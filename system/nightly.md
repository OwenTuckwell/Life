# Nightly control — the two messages (22:00 + 22:30)

These are the heart of the system: every night, two messages that (a) close out today and (b) set tomorrow up so there are **no decisions left to make in the morning**. Owen wanted these specifically — a plan *and* a check-in, not the same canned routine every day.

Owen parts from his girlfriend ~22:00–22:30, so this lands right as his evening winds down.

---

## 22:00 — Plan + check-in
The main message. Two jobs: find out how today went, and hand him tomorrow decided.

**Before writing it, gather:**
- Recent `progress/log.md` entries (last 2–3 days — what's the momentum, did he miss anything, is a "never miss twice" flag live?).
- `system/one-offs.md` for anything unusual about tomorrow (early start, moved tee time, ref game, day off, plans) — this **overrides** the default weekly shape.
- `habits/weekly-schedule.md` for the baseline shape of that weekday.
- `habits/health-data.md` for any sleep/steps figures.

**The message (keep it phone-length — a tight, scannable brief, not an essay):**

1. **Check-in (ask, don't assume):** "How did today go?" — nudge for: did you move / gym done, energy, and anything that slipped, plus his Fitbit numbers (Readiness, Sleep score, Sleep time, Steps, Resting HR — he reads them off the Fitbit app, or sends them via the FitForge "Copy my day" paste). One line. His reply gets logged (see below), and **low Readiness / poor sleep softens tomorrow's movement; high Readiness green-lights pushing** (see `habits/health-data.md`).
2. **🌙 Asleep by / wake:** the numbers, explicitly. Default **lights out 23:00 → wake 07:00** (~8h). Hold wake at 07:00 seven days a week — it's the keystone. Only shift for a real reason logged in `one-offs.md` (e.g. early start, early tee time) and say why.
3. **🏋️ Tomorrow's movement:** state it plainly — **walk / run / gym**. Decision rules below.
4. **⏱️ Tomorrow's shape:** the day's anchors from the weekly schedule + any `one-offs.md` override (leave-for-work time, gym, golf tee + leave time, Dad's, ref game). Just the anchors, not a timetable.
5. **🌙 Night-before prep (the real secret):** "Do it now: lay out clothes, pack the bag, kit + trainers by the door, fill the water bottle." Half of a chaotic morning is solved here.

Then **push a short teaser** to the phone (e.g. "🌙 Tomorrow's set — asleep by 23:00, wake 07:00, [movement]. How did today go?"), with the full brief in the session for when he taps in.

### Deciding tomorrow's movement (rule: **fixed gym days, flex the rest**)
- **Gym days are fixed: Thu / Sat / Sun.** On these → **Gym**, and name the workout. A/B rotation: gym days run A/B/A one week, B/A/B the next, alternating. Check the last gym entry in the log to see which is next.
- **Every other day, choose walk / run / rest** using load:
  - **Wed (golf 9) and Fri (golf 18)** already carry big cardio → **easy walk or rest**, never a run stacked on top.
  - **Mon / Tue (work days)** → **walk** by default (10k steps, morning). Offer a **run only if**: he's fresh, hasn't already run 1–2× this week, and tomorrow isn't next to a heavy day. Max **1–2 runs/week** — daily running is over-reach and triggers his slip cascade.
  - If the last 1–2 days were heavy or he reported being wrecked/sore → **rest or gentle walk**, and say so. Recovery is a valid call, not a miss.
  - A weekend **ref game** counts as real activity (2+ hrs on feet) — treat it like a cardio day and keep other movement easy.
- Always give **one** clear call, with a one-line why ("Golf tomorrow, so just an easy walk — save the legs").

### Logging his reply (the check-in loop)
When he replies about his day, **append an entry to `progress/log.md`** (newest at top) in the existing format: date, what he did, a win, a struggle, tomorrow's focus, and a short coach note. Then, if relevant, update `habits/tracker.md`. This log is what tomorrow's brief reads — it's the memory, so it must be written.
- If he **doesn't reply by the 22:30 message**, that's fine — log "no check-in" and carry the plan forward. Missing the *reply* isn't a slip; just don't let the plan itself lapse.

---

## 22:30 — Wind-down lock
Short, firm, one-way. This replaces the old "set your own 22:45 alarm."

- **Gather:** did he reply to the 22:00 check-in? What wake time did the 22:00 brief set?
- **The message:** "🌙 Wind down now. Lights out by 23:00 to hit 07:00. Phone on charge, out of reach. Never miss twice." Push it.
- If he **still hasn't checked in**, tack on a 6-word nudge ("...and how did today go?") so there's one last easy chance to log the day.
- Keep it under two lines. At 22:30 he doesn't need words, he needs the light off.

---

## Tone
Calm, direct, a bit of warmth — a coach who's on his side, not a drill sergeant and not a hype-bot. Short sentences. Name the one thing that matters tonight. Never guilt-trip a miss; point at the next action. The whole system runs on him trusting that the message is worth reading.
